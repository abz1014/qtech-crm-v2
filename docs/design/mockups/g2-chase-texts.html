How the text is built

  • One message carries every open question, numbered, so the customer can answer by number.
  • Questions are the ones you captured. The app never invents a question, an answer or a spec.
  • The confirm-by date defaults to one working day before the bid and is editable. With no bid date it falls back to three working days from today.
  • WhatsApp stays short: a reminder line, the questions, the date. Email adds the subject, the context and an offer to talk it through.
  • Copying stamps the chase. The RFQ moves to Waiting, and from the third ask the app suggests closing as no reply.
  • Sender name and number come from the signed-in user. Tone is "gentle reminder" on a second ask, never "overdue".

How the text is built

  • Only items with no quote and no "cannot offer" from this supplier appear. Items they already priced are shown greyed and left out.
  • The reply-by date defaults to two working days before our bid and is editable.
  • The text asks for price and lead time, and invites a short "cannot offer", which the app records as no offer.
  • The customer, their budget and our margin are never mentioned.
  • Copying stamps chase 1. From the second chase the app suggests floating to another supplier, listing the next suggestions with their memory lines.
  • Email repeats the item lines in full and adds availability to the ask.
